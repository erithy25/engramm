A chat assistant that shows its sources.

ENGRAMM answers from Wikipedia and Wikidata, cites a source for every fact and says “I don’t know” instead of making things up. It remembers what you tell it — and forgets it for real when you ask. It runs on your computer, offline, without an account and without a neural network.

Download for free

Sources, not guesses

Every fact comes with the article or database entry it is from. Same question, same answer.

Memory you control

See everything ENGRAMM knows about you in one list. Delete an entry and it is gone — provably.

Private by design

No servers, no telemetry, no account. After installing the knowledge pack it never goes online.

Runs on modest PCs

About 0.8 GB of memory with the lite pack. No graphics card needed.

Download

The app is small; on first start it asks before downloading its knowledge pack (lite ≈ 0.7 GB, standard ≈ 1.4 GB), or uses a pack folder you already have.

What ENGRAMM can’t do

ENGRAMM uses counting, rules and small linear models — no neural network. That makes it transparent and light, and it also sets hard limits:

  • It is not as good as ChatGPT at open conversation, explanations or writing. We measure this in blind tests and publish the results, including the bad ones.
  • It only knows what is in its knowledge pack. Anything else gets an honest “I don’t know”.
  • Writing help covers routine texts (emails, requests, short notes, summaries), not creative work.
  • English first; German is in progress.
